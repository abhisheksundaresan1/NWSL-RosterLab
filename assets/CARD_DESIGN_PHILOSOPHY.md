# Broadcast Dossier
### A design philosophy for the RosterLab player card

---

**The philosophy.** Athletic performance is usually rendered either as spectacle or as spreadsheet — the roar or the raw table. *Broadcast Dossier* refuses both. It treats a player's season as a **document of record**: something observed carefully, measured honestly, and set down with the composure of a technical archive. The visual language borrows from two disciplines that rarely meet — the confident, condensed typography of stadium signage and broadcast lower-thirds, and the systematic restraint of a scientific field report. The result should feel like evidence, not advertising. Nothing shouts, because nothing needs to: the numbers are the argument, and the design's only job is to give them authority.

**Space and form.** The composition is governed by a strict vertical rhythm and a single generous margin held without exception, so the eye always knows where the edge of the world is. Content is organised into anchored zones — identity, verdict, evidence, interpretation, provenance — separated not by boxes or borders but by **rules and silence**. Panels and containers are avoided; a hairline and empty space do the same work with far more grace. Critically, negative space must always read as *deliberate*, never as absence. A gap the designer chose is elegant; a gap left by content that ran out is a defect. Every zone is therefore anchored top and bottom, and slack is distributed, never pooled.

**Colour and material.** The base is a near-black navy — not decorative, but a darkroom in which a single colour can matter. That colour is the club's own, and it is the **only** accent permitted: it draws the bars, marks the hero figure, and rules the edge of the canvas. One accent, used repeatedly and without dilution, is what separates a designed object from a chart with opinions. Supporting information recedes through desaturated blue-greys in a disciplined three-step ladder — primary, secondary, tertiary — and never through a second hue. Where a value falls below average, it is expressed through **direction and weight**, not through alarm; there is no red, because a below-average figure is information, not an error.

**Scale and rhythm.** Hierarchy is established by dramatic, unambiguous contrast rather than timid gradation. The player's name and her value score are enormous, set in a condensed display face that carries weight without consuming width. Everything else is small, quiet, and precise. Between the largest and smallest element there should be a ratio of roughly seven to one — a jump big enough that the eye never has to deliberate about what matters first. Structural micro-labels are set in uppercase monospace, generously letterspaced, functioning as the ruled headings of a technical form: they organise without competing. Numerals live in a monospaced face so that columns align to the pixel and figures read as measurements rather than decoration.

**Composition and evidence.** Data is drawn, never plotted. Axes, tick marks, gridlines and legends — the furniture of software output — are stripped away entirely, and each bar is labelled directly at its head and tail, so the chart is read the way a sentence is read. A single hairline records the positional average, present for anyone who looks for it and invisible to anyone who does not. This is the discipline that most separates the amateur from the master: the willingness to delete every mark that is not carrying meaning, and to spend the reclaimed space on clarity.

**Craftsmanship.** This object must survive two hostile contexts: the thumbnail, where it is 400 pixels wide in a scrolling feed, and the projector, where every misalignment is magnified. It therefore permits no text below the legibility floor, no low-contrast grey on grey, no element within a hair of the margin, and no collision anywhere. **Every edge is deliberate, every baseline is intentional, every interval is measured.** The finished card should look like the product of countless hours by someone at the absolute top of their field — the sort of restraint that reads as effortless precisely because it was laboured over. If an instinct arises to add an ornament, a gradient, a badge, or a flourish, the correct response is to refine what already exists until it is crisper. Nothing decorative. Everything considered.
